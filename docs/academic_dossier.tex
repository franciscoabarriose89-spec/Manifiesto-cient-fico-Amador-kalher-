\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a formal academic framework for the study of arithmetic decomposition and a generalized Amador-Kähler coherence index. The objective is to construct a mathematically disciplined model that allows for rigorous symbolic reasoning, reproducible numerical evaluation, and conceptual extension across scientific domains. We define a decomposition measure based on quadratic energy and total magnitude, and construct a bounded coherence functional that remains interpretable under uniform regimes. The results establish positivity, boundedness, and stability conditions for admissible positive sequences. This version includes a dedicated experimental methods section and complete appendices suitable for international academic review, computational replication, and formal scientific documentation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} arithmetic decomposition, Amador-Kähler framework, coherence index, mathematical formalization, scientific methodology, reproducible proof, experimental methods, technical appendices.

\section{Introduction}
The study of structure, measure, and formal representation lies at the heart of scientific reasoning. In particular, arithmetic operations provide the elementary substrate from which empirical, formal, and computational systems are interpreted. Yet, the deeper relevance of arithmetic is not limited to the mechanics of numerical calculation. It also concerns the conditions under which representation becomes meaningful, stable, and explanatory.

This paper introduces a formal model for arithmetic decomposition as a measure of internal energetic concentration and combines it with a generalized Amador-Kähler coherence functional. The proposed framework is intentionally simple, yet sufficiently rich to support rigorous statements about positivity, boundedness, and uniform-regime behavior. It offers both a conceptual and computational basis for scientific inquiry in domains where formal structure must be balanced against interpretability.

The present version extends the original framework with an explicit experimental methodology section and complete appendices. This is intended to align the work more closely with international standards for reproducibility, computational validation, and transparent scholarly communication.

\section{Formal Foundation}
Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be an admissible vector in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$. Define the vector mass and quadratic energy by
\begin{equation}
T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i, \qquad E(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}
The arithmetic decomposition index of $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{E(x)}{T(x)+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The additive term $+1$ prevents degeneracy in the low-mass regime and preserves regularity when the total mass is close to zero.

\begin{definition}
The Amador-Kähler coherence index is defined by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}(x)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{D}(x)}{\max\{1,n\}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This index maps the decomposition measure into a bounded interval in $[0,1)$.

\section{Methodological Principles}
The construction rests on four principles.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Positivity.} All admissible magnitudes are strictly positive.
\item \textbf{Boundedness.} The coherence measure is normalized into the unit interval.
\item \textbf{Stability.} The model remains regular under repeated uniform values.
\item \textbf{Interoperability.} The formalism can be implemented and tested in multiple languages and research environments.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Methods}
\subsection{Goal of the experimental design}
The experimental method is designed to validate the mathematical framework under controlled numerical conditions. The objective is to test whether the theoretical claims about positivity, boundedness, and uniform-regime behavior are preserved in actual computational evaluation.

\subsection{Input construction}
For each test, a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$ is generated or specified explicitly. The method distinguishes between two classes of inputs:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{General positive vectors.} Examples include nonuniform sequences of positive values such as $x=(1,2,3,4)$ and other random positive configurations.
\item \textbf{Uniform vectors.} Special cases of the form $x=(c,\ldots,c)$ for constant $c>0$ and size $n$.
\end{enumerate}

The uniform regime is particularly important because it enables analytic verification of the model's behavior.

\subsection{Quantities measured}
For each vector $x$, the following quantities are computed:
\begin{equation}
T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i,
\qquad
E(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2,
\qquad
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{E(x)}{T(x)+1},
\qquad
\mathcal{K}(x)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{D}(x)}{\max\{1,n\}}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Validation criteria}
A configuration is considered valid when the following computational checks hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item $\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0$;
\item $0\le\mathcal{K}(x)<1$;
\item in the uniform regime, $\mathcal{D}(x)=\dfrac{nc^2}{nc+1}$;
\item the measured index increases monotonically with the parameter $c$ in the uniform family.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Computational implementation}
The numerical experiments are executed in both Python and C++ using the same mathematical definitions. The independent implementation in two languages reduces the risk of language-specific numerical artifacts and strengthens reproducibility.

The algorithmic sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item read or define the positive vector;
\item compute $T(x)$ and $E(x)$;
\item evaluate $\mathcal{D}(x)$ and $\mathcal{K}(x)$;
\item validate the inequalities and the uniform formula;
\item report the results in tabular form.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Core Theoretical Results}
\begin{lemma}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, we have $T(x)>0$ and $E(x)\ge 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since each component satisfies $x_i>0$, their sum is strictly positive and their squared values are non-negative. Therefore $T(x)>0$ and $E(x)=\sum_i x_i^2\ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, the arithmetic decomposition index satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From Lemma 1, $E(x)\ge 0$ and $T(x)+1>0$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{E(x)}{T(x)+1}\ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, the Amador-Kähler coherence index satisfies
\begin{equation}
0\le \mathcal{K}(x) < 1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{\mathcal{D}(x)}{\max\{1,n\}}.
\end{equation}
By Theorem 1, $\alpha\ge 0$. Hence $e^{-\alpha}\in (0,1]$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}(x)=1-e^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Since $e^{-\alpha}>0$, we have $\mathcal{K}(x)<1$. Since $e^{-\alpha}\le 1$, we also have $\mathcal{K}(x)\ge 0$. Therefore $0\le\mathcal{K}(x)<1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
If $x=(c,\ldots,c)$ with $n$ identical positive entries, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{n c^2}{n c + 1}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, this quantity is strictly increasing in $c$ for every fixed $n\ge 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If all components equal $c$, then
\begin{equation}
E(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^2 = n c^2,
\qquad
T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c = n c.
\end{equation}
Hence,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{n c^2}{n c +1}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $c$ yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dc}\mathcal{D}(x)
=\frac{n c (n c + 2)}{(n c + 1)^2}>0,
\end{equation}
which proves strict monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
In the uniform regime, the coherence index $\mathcal{K}(x)$ is also monotone increasing in $c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal{K}(x)=1-e^{-\mathcal{D}(x)/\max\{1,n\}}$ and the exponential map is monotone decreasing, increasing $\mathcal{D}(x)$ increases $\mathcal{K}(x)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scientific Interpretation}
The formalism above offers a compact model for assessing how dispersed or concentrated a positive vector is relative to its total mass. The quantity $E(x)$ acts as a quadratic energy term, while $T(x)+1$ regulates the scaling to preserve regularity. The resulting ratio captures the tendency of the system to concentrate magnitude within a few components, while the coherence index compresses this quantity into a normalized score.

This interpretation is useful in settings where one wants to compare systems of different sizes but comparable structure. The model is especially natural in formal scientific workflows that require a scalar descriptor with well-defined bounds, monotonicity, and computational reproducibility.

\section{Technical Appendix A: Formal derivation of the decomposition measure}
Let $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$. The arithmetic decomposition index is constructed from the quadratic energy $E(x)$ and the total magnitude $T(x)$. The denominator uses $T(x)+1$ instead of $T(x)$ alone to avoid singular behavior near zero and to ensure numerical continuity in low-mass regimes. This design preserves three desirable properties:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item non-negativity;
\item bounded regularization;
\item stability under uniform scaling in the positive domain.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting model is therefore appropriate for scientific analysis when both magnitude and concentration are relevant variables.

\section{Technical Appendix B: Computational reproducibility}
The computational experiments used the following logic:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Compute $T(x)=\sum_i x_i$;
\item Compute $E(x)=\sum_i x_i^2$;
\item Evaluate $\mathcal{D}(x)=E(x)/(T(x)+1)$;
\item Evaluate $\mathcal{K}(x)=1-\exp(-\mathcal{D}(x)/\max\{1,n\})$;
\item Check the theoretical constraints $\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0$ and $0\le\mathcal{K}(x)<1$.
\end{enumerate}

The same logic is implemented in both Python and C++, which allows cross-language verification and improves confidence in the final results.

\section{Technical Appendix C: Reproducibility protocol}
The following protocol was used to conduct the numerical verification:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Define a positive vector $x$.
\item Evaluate $T(x)$ and $E(x)$ using standard exact arithmetic or double precision numerical computation.
\item Apply the formulae for $\mathcal{D}(x)$ and $\mathcal{K}(x)$ directly.
\item Compare against the identities stated in the propositions.
\item Record the outputs in tabular form for reproducibility.
\end{enumerate}

This protocol is intentionally minimal, transparent, and language-agnostic so that it can be independently replicated by other researchers.
