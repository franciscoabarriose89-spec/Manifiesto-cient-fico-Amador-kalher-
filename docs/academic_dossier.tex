\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces a compact mathematical framework for analyzing arithmetic decomposition and a generalized Amador-Kähler coherence functional. The goal is to provide a formally transparent model that supports rigorous symbolic reasoning, reproducible numerical testing, and conceptual extension across scientific disciplines. We define the decomposition index as a normalized quadratic-energy measure over positive vectors and derive a bounded coherence score in the unit interval. The formal results establish non-negativity, boundedness, and monotone behavior under uniform configurations. The framework is intentionally simple, yet suitable for academic review, computational replication, and further theoretical generalization.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} arithmetic decomposition, Amador-Kähler framework, coherence index, mathematical formalization, reproducible research, preprint, numerical validation.

\section{Introduction}
The formalization of knowledge requires a careful balance between symbolic rigor and interpretive flexibility. In scientific practice, even the most elementary objects—such as positive magnitude vectors and arithmetic aggregates—can be reinterpreted as structural evidences of deeper conceptual organization. This observation is central to the present work.

The objective of this preprint is to present a mathematically coherent framework for measuring internal concentration and coherence in positive data structures. To this end, we define an arithmetic decomposition metric based on quadratic energy and total magnitude, and we embed it in a bounded coherence functional inspired by the Amador-Kähler perspective. The resulting formulation is simple enough to remain transparent, while sufficiently structured to support formal claims and computational verification.

This document is intentionally written in a preprint style: it is concise, formal, and oriented toward scientific evaluation. It gives complete definitions, rigorous statements, and reproducible validation procedures suitable for review in an international research context.

\section{Formal framework}
Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be an admissible vector in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$. Define
\begin{equation}
T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i, \qquad E(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}
The arithmetic decomposition index of $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{E(x)}{T(x)+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The additive unit in the denominator ensures regularity in low-mass regimes and prevents pathological degeneracy when the sum of the components is small.

\begin{definition}
The Amador-Kähler coherence index is defined by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}(x)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{D}(x)}{\max\{1,n\}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This produces a bounded score in the interval $[0,1)$, allowing direct comparison across different positive vectors.

\section{Methodological principles}
The model is grounded in four principles.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Positivity.} All admissible magnitudes are strictly positive.
\item \textbf{Boundedness.} The coherence functional remains normalized in the unit interval.
\item \textbf{Stability.} The model preserves regularity in uniform regimes.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The method can be implemented and verified in different computational languages.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Theoretical results}
\begin{lemma}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, we have $T(x)>0$ and $E(x)\ge 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each component satisfies $x_i>0$, hence $T(x)=\sum_i x_i>0$ and $x_i^2\ge 0$ for all $i$. Summing yields $E(x)\ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, the arithmetic decomposition index satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From the previous lemma, $E(x)\ge 0$ and $T(x)+1>0$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{E(x)}{T(x)+1}\ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For every admissible vector $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$, the coherence index satisfies
\begin{equation}
0\le \mathcal{K}(x)<1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{\mathcal{D}(x)}{\max\{1,n\}}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0$, we have $\alpha\ge 0$. Thus $e^{-\alpha}\in (0,1]$, and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}(x)=1-e^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal{K}(x)\ge 0$ and $\mathcal{K}(x)<1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
If $x=(c,\ldots,c)$ with $n$ identical positive entries, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{n c^2}{n c +1}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, this quantity is strictly increasing in $c$ for each fixed $n\ge 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $E(x)=nc^2$ and $T(x)=nc$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{nc^2}{nc+1}.
\end{equation}
Differentiation yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dc}\mathcal{D}(x)=\frac{nc(nc+2)}{(nc+1)^2}>0.
\end{equation}
Hence the decomposition index is strictly increasing in $c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
In the uniform regime, the coherence index $\mathcal{K}(x)$ is also monotone increasing in $c$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal{K}(x)=1-e^{-\mathcal{D}(x)/\max\{1,n\}}$, an increase in $\mathcal{D}(x)$ produces an increase in $\mathcal{K}(x)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Experimental validation}
The model was evaluated with positive vectors of different sizes and configurations. The computations aim to confirm the theoretical claims under both general and uniform conditions.

\subsection{Validation criteria}
Each test verifies the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item $\mathcal{D}(x)\ge 0$;
\item $0\le \mathcal{K}(x)<1$;
\item for uniform vectors: $\mathcal{D}(c,\ldots,c)=\dfrac{nc^2}{nc+1}$;
\item the uniform family is monotone in $c$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Interpretation and significance}
The proposed framework can be understood as a mathematically robust measure of relative concentration within a positive system. The quadratic-energy term captures internal dispersion, while the regularized denominator prevents singular behavior at small mass. The coherence index then transforms this raw energetic measure into a normalized score that is easier to compare across systems of different size.

This is particularly relevant in scientific settings where the objective is not merely to observe variation, but to express it in a bounded, interpretable, and reproducible form. The methodology remains deliberately compact, but it supports formal critique, practical computation, and future generalization.

\section{Conclusion}
This preprint provides a minimal yet rigorous framework for arithmetic decomposition and coherence assessment. It establishes key mathematical properties, demonstrates computational validation, and preserves conceptual clarity. The model is intentionally simple and transparent, which makes it suitable for further extension in mathematical, computational, and interdisciplinary research contexts.

The contribution is not intended as a static doctrine, but as a formalized working foundation that can be refined, generalized, and tested by the scientific community. The resulting structure is thus both academically useful and operationally reproducible.

\section{Appendix A. Technical derivation}
Let $x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$. The decomposition index is built from the pair $(E(x), T(x))$, where $E(x)$ measures quadratic concentration and $T(x)$ measures total positive mass. The denominator is regularized as $T(x)+1$ to retain stability near zero and to avoid degenerate low-mass singularities. This gives a deterministic measure with interpretive relevance in both theoretical and computational settings.

\section{Appendix B. Reproducibility protocol}
The numerical checks follow a direct protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item select or construct a strictly positive vector $x$;
\item compute $T(x)$ and $E(x)$;
\item evaluate $\mathcal{D}(x)$ and $\mathcal{K}(x)$;
\item verify the inequalities and uniform formulas;
\item record the numerical results.
\end{enumerate}
